# Seven Kingdoms Population and Economy Design Notes

Snapshot: 2026-05-10

## Purpose

This is a weak comparative reference for
[Vespasian Housing Progression Design Notes](../vespasian_housing_progression_design_notes.md).
Seven Kingdoms is useful because it links population, jobs, taxation, loyalty,
food, military recruitment, and political assimilation.

## Useful Patterns

Seven Kingdoms treats villages as political and economic units. Peasants can
work in firms, pay taxes while living in villages, be conscripted into forts,
and be affected by loyalty/resistance. The manual notes that soldiers no longer
live in villages and that a large army restricts the tax base because only
villagers pay taxes. It also describes job-providing firms as a way to reduce
independent village resistance when trying to absorb them.

That is a different genre, but the systemic triangle is useful:

- jobs make a realm attractive or persuasive
- taxation extracts money but risks loyalty
- military or specialist assignment removes people from the village economy
- idle or ordinary villagers remain economically important

## Possible Vespasian Lessons

| Seven Kingdoms pattern | Vespasian use |
| --- | --- |
| Jobs affect political attraction | Employment opportunities could generate housing demand, not only consume labor. |
| Taxes interact with loyalty | Market revenue and class taxes could affect sentiment or corruption. |
| Soldiers/specialists leave tax base | Patrician or military assignments can be useful while reducing another resource. |
| Villages need economic links before assimilation | Native/tribal settlements could Romanize through jobs, markets, forts, and services. |
| Workers can be replaced by better-skilled workers | Future classed employment could include skill/education quality, not only headcount. |

## Cautions

Seven Kingdoms is an RTS empire game, not a city-builder. Its most useful
pattern is the idea that employment, taxes, and political loyalty are coupled.
Do not use its numbers or pacing directly.
